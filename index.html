Is an Online Casino Philippines Legal?

Yes. An online casino Philippines legal operation runs under a license issued by the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation (PAGCOR). The distinction is important: it's not that online gambling itself is illegal in the Philippines. What the law regulates is where and how those bets are placed. Who Regulates Online Casinos in the Philippines?

PAGCOR — the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation — is the primary government authority overseeing all gaming activity in the Philippines. It was established in 1976 under Presidential Decree 1067-A and operates as a 100% government-owned corporation under the Office of the President.

PAGCOR's Core Responsibilities

PAGCOR licenses and supervises all major gaming verticals: land-based casinos, bingo parlors, electronic gaming cafes, and domestic online platforms. It sets technical standards, enforces KYC and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) compliance, and handles formal player disputes through its official player assistance channel.

CEZA and APECO — Secondary Zones

The Cagayan Economic Zone Authority (CEZA) and the Aurora Pacific Economic Zone (APECO) can also issue gaming licenses within their special economic zones. Historically, these jurisdictions issued offshore-facing licenses. With the Anti-POGO Act now in force, the relevance of these zones for domestic-facing online casino operations is significantly reduced.

The Anti-POGO Act of 2025: What Changed and Why It Matters

Republic Act No. 12312, the Anti-POGO Act of 2025, permanently banned all offshore gaming operations in the Philippines. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. signed it into law on October 23, 2025, making permanent a ban he first imposed via Executive Order 74 in November 2024.

Under RA 12312, the following are now expressly prohibited:

  • Establishing or operating any offshore gaming platform in the Philippines
  • Accepting bets for offshore gaming operations
  • Acting as a POGO gaming content or service provider
  • Leasing property for offshore gaming use
  • Possessing gaming equipment associated with offshore operations

Penalties are severe. First-time offenders face 6 to 8 years imprisonment and fines between ₱300,000 and ₱15 million. Third-offense penalties reach up to 12 years imprisonment and a ₱50 million fine. Foreign nationals convicted under the law are deported after serving their sentence and permanently barred from re-entering the Philippines.

Pro tip: The Anti-POGO Act does not affect domestically licensed platforms. If a casino holds a valid PAGCOR Interactive Gaming Licence (IGL) and serves Filipino players within the Philippines, it continues to operate legally. The law targeted offshore operators — not the local licensed market Filipino players use every day.

Legal Requirements for Filipino Players

Licensed casinos enforce this through KYC verification. Accepted documents include:

KYC is a one-time process per platform. Once your documents are reviewed — typically within 24 to 48 hours — your verification status is permanent unless you change account details. Submit your documents at registration, not when you're ready to withdraw.

Taxes on Online Casino Winnings in the Philippines

Filipino players who win on a PAGCOR-licensed platform keep 100% of their winnings. Gambling winnings from licensed operators are exempt from personal income tax under Philippine law. The tax obligation falls entirely on the casino operator, not the player.

This exemption applies only to PAGCOR-licensed operations. If you play on an unlicensed site, there is no clear legal framework protecting those winnings — and potential tax exposure can arise from unexplained income without a licensed source.
